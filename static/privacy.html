What is stored on your device

This site sets no cookies. It keeps these values in your browser's local storage so the choices you made survive a reload. That is the whole list: none of it is sent back to the server, and none of it can follow you to another site.

Alongside those settings the browser keeps an offline copy of the page, its scripts and the weather data you last loaded, so the site still opens on a crowded convention network — and so that opening it costs the little machine behind it nothing. That copy holds only what you had already downloaded, it is never sent anywhere, and it is read by nobody but your own browser.

Clearing site data for this domain removes all of it — settings and offline copy alike — and costs you nothing but your preferences.

What the server holds

Your browser has to tell the server its IP address to receive a reply at all. Here is everything that happens with it:

  • Rate limiting. The address is held in memory for one minute, so a runaway script cannot crowd out everyone else. It is never written to disk.
  • The visitor count. Not the address itself, but an unreadable hash of it — see below.
  • No request log. The access log, which would record a line per request including your address, is switched off. The application log keeps errors and the occasional "someone is hammering the API" line, and that line names the hash rather than the address.

The count exists so the page can admit the machine is busy instead of leaving you at a spinner — this site runs on a single computer at home. Your address is hashed with a random secret that is regenerated every time the server starts, so the same visitor gets a different hash after a restart and the two cannot be matched. The hash cannot be turned back into an address, it is dropped once you have been quiet for a few minutes, and it is used for exactly one thing: taking the length of the list. The total is public at /api/load as a load signal rather than an audience figure — everyone behind one shared network counts once, and a crawler counts as a person.

There is no database, no profile and no analytics store. Nothing about a visit outlives the process it happened in: a restart wipes all of it. The remaining log is capped at 30 MB and rolls over.

Who else sees your address

Three parties see your IP address in the course of loading this page. None is under this site's control, and each has its own policy:

  • Cloudflare carries all traffic through a tunnel and terminates the encryption, so it sees every request, and it may set a cookie of its own (typically __cf_bm) to tell automated traffic from people. That storage is Cloudflare's, not this site's. Cloudflare Inc. is based in the United States; their privacy policy applies to it.
  • CARTO (basemaps.cartocdn.com) serves the map background on the radar and model maps.
  • Deutscher Wetterdienst (maps.dwd.de) serves the radar and warning tiles.

Your browser fetches those tiles directly; everything else — the map library, the styles, the scripts, the video — comes from this machine, and the weather data itself is fetched by the server from DWD OpenData rather than by you. There are no web fonts, no advertising, no social buttons and no embedded third-party content beyond those tiles.

Why there is no consent box

Because there is nothing to consent to. A consent banner is for storage on your device that you did not ask for — tracking, advertising, analytics — and there is none here. What this site stores are the settings you chose yourself, the "strictly necessary for a service you requested" case that German TDDDG § 25 (2) exempts. Asking permission for that would be theatre, and would train you to click through the banners that matter. So you get a notice instead of a gate: shown once, dismissible, and the page works either way.

The legal basis for the two things that do touch an address — the rate limit and the visitor count — is legitimate interest, GDPR Art. 6 (1) (f): keeping a small self-hosted site reachable and telling you honestly when it is struggling.

Your rights

Under the GDPR you may ask for access to the data held about you, its correction or erasure, a restriction on its use, a copy of it, or object to it being processed at all (Art. 15–21). Write to the address below. You may also complain to a data protection supervisory authority in the country where you live or work.

In practice there will be very little to hand over, and that is the point: nothing here is stored under your name, and neither the address in memory nor the visitor hash can be traced back to you — not even by the person running the site.
